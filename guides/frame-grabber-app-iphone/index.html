What is a frame grabber?

A frame grabber is an app that pulls individual still images (frames) out of a video and saves them as photos. Since iOS 27, the Photos app can save one paused frame with Save Video Frame as Photo. A dedicated app is still worth it on older iOS, and when you need many frames, PNG files, or a ZIP or PDF of stills.

6 things to look for

  1. Precise frame selection. A filmstrip or scrubber that lets you land on the exact moment, not just the nearest second.
  2. Real frame extraction. The image should come from the video file, not a picture of the screen. Test it: grab a frame from a landscape 4K clip and check the image size.
  3. On-device processing. Your family videos shouldn't need an upload. Check the App Privacy label and try it in airplane mode.
  4. Batch grabbing. Storyboards, sports and tutorials need many frames. Tapping once per frame and exporting once is a huge time-saver.
  5. Formats and containers. JPG, PNG and HEIC cover every use. ZIP and PDF export are rare but useful for sending sets of frames.
  6. Fair pricing. Look at what's free, what needs a subscription, and whether there's a one-time option.

How to test a frame grabber app in two minutes

  1. Open a real video

    Use a landscape clip with movement. In Video To Photo – Grab Frame, share it from Photos and it opens straight into frame-grab mode.

  2. Try to hit one exact frame

    Scrub to a blink or a ball in mid-air. The filmstrip plus large preview should make that easy — tap Grab This Frame.

  3. Grab several and review

    Grab four or five frames, then preview and zoom. You should be able to delete misses before exporting.

  4. Export and check the file

    Export as PNG and open the image info in Photos. The dimensions should match the video, not your screen.

Try it on your own video — it takes about a minute.

When you don't need an app

If you're on iOS 27 and need a single frame, use Photos: pause the video, tap ••• and choose Save Video Frame as Photo. On older iOS, a screenshot is fine for a rough, one-off image.
