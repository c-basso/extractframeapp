How to extract multiple frames from a video (on your iPhone)

Storyboards, sports sequences, before-and-after shots, reaction sets — sometimes one frame isn't enough. Here's how to pull out as many frames as you need in one go, right on your iPhone.

Quick answer

Open the video in Video To Photo – Grab Frame and tap Grab This Frame at every moment you want. Review the set, delete the misses, then export them all at once as separate JPG, PNG or HEIC images, a single ZIP archive, or a single PDF.

Do you need all frames, or the right ones?

A 30-second clip at 30 fps is 900 frames. Exporting every single one (the desktop approach with tools like ffmpeg or VLC) leaves you with hundreds of near-identical files to sort through. On a phone, it's almost always faster to pick the frames that matter and export just those.

GoalBest approach
Storyboard or lesson stepsGrab one frame per scene or step
Sports / motion sequenceGrab frames at even gaps through the movement
Find the perfect shotGrab 3–5 neighbouring frames, keep the sharpest
Every frame for animation or analysisDesktop tools (ffmpeg) are better suited

How to extract multiple frames from a video on iPhone

  1. Open the clip

    Share the video from Photos to the app, or open Video To Photo – Grab Frame and pick it.

  2. Grab every moment you need

    Scrub along the filmstrip and tap Grab This Frame at each moment. The tray fills up and the counter shows how many frames you have.

  3. Review the set

    Preview frames full screen, zoom to check focus, and remove duplicates or blurry ones.

  4. Export them together

    Pick JPEG, PNG or HEIC, then choose Files for separate images, ZIP Archive for one download, or PDF for a single document.

Try it on your own video — it takes about a minute.

ZIP, PDF or separate files?

  • Separate files — best when you'll edit or post frames individually.
  • ZIP archive — one tidy file to AirDrop, email or upload to a shared drive. The recipient unzips and gets every frame.
  • PDF — your selected frames in one document. Great for storyboards, class notes and client reviews. See turning video frames into a PDF.

Tips for sequences

  • For a motion sequence, grab frames at roughly even gaps so the movement reads smoothly.
  • Use PNG if you'll combine frames into a collage or composite later — it avoids repeated JPEG compression.
  • Save the project. You can reopen the clip later to add frames you missed.

Frequently asked questions

How many frames can I grab from one video?

Grab as many moments as you need — tap Grab This Frame for each one — then export the whole set together. Larger sets simply take a little longer to export.

Can I extract every single frame automatically?

Video To Photo – Grab Frame is designed for choosing frames. If you truly need every frame for animation or analysis, a desktop tool such as ffmpeg is the better fit.

How do I send lots of frames at once?

Export as a ZIP archive and share the single file via AirDrop, Mail or Files. Or export a PDF so the frames stay in order.
